TCS TwinX — Digital Twin Decision Platform

RoleLead UX Designer
Team8 designers
Timeframe2021–2024
ToolsFigma, Principle
Context

TwinX lets enterprise operations teams simulate the downstream effect of a decision — a staffing change, a supply reroute — before making it, using actor-model computation to model how thousands of interdependent processes would respond.

Problem

Early prototypes showed simulation results as raw data tables. Operations leads couldn't tell whether a result was good news without cross-referencing three other screens.

Process & decisions
  • Redesigned the results view around comparison, not raw output — every simulation shows against a baseline by default.
  • Cut the onboarding flow from 12 steps to 4 after usability testing with 6 operations leads.
  • Introduced a confidence indicator so leads could tell simulated-but-uncertain results from validated ones.
Fig. 02 — comparison view, v3Placeholder art
Outcome
40%↓Time to interpret a result
6Ops leads in validation testing

Illustrative figures for this mockup — real outcome metrics get sourced from Muthukumar during Phase 14 content work, not fabricated here.

Reflection

The comparison-first view was the right call, but it took two rounds of testing to get there — the first version still asked leads to hold the baseline in their head. If I started over, I'd usability-test the baseline framing before touching visual design at all.

About

I design for the moment someone has to trust a system they can't fully see.

Ten years in, most of what I've shipped sits behind a login: enterprise dashboards, simulation tools, banking infrastructure — software judged by whether people trust it under pressure, not by how it photographs. That's shaped how I work: fewer flourishes, more usability testing, and a habit of asking what a screen has to prove before I decide what it should look like.

I've spent the last several years leading design for platforms at TCS and McKinsey Global Services, and mentoring the designers who came up alongside me.
