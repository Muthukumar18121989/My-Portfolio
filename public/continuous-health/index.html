01 · The problem

Health tracking stops
when the device does.

Every wearable record has holes in it. Not because the sensors fail — because the device has to come off.

  • Worn — 18 hours. Apple’s own figure for a day of normal use on Apple Watch SE 3.
  • Asleep — 6 hours. On Series 11, the quoted 24 hours includes these six. Sleep tracking doesn’t extend the day; it consumes it.
  • Charging — the gap. 30–75 minutes that has to come out of the same cycle. Whatever happens in it is not recorded.
Behaviour

The watch comes off

For charging, for a shower, for a formal watch, for a night when you just don’t want it. Each removal is a hole in the record.

Consequence

Baselines get noisy

A trend built on intermittent data is a weaker trend. Gaps don’t just lose hours — they degrade everything inferred from the surrounding ones.

Response

People stop bothering

Roughly a third of owners abandon the device altogether — not from breakage, but because it never became useful enough to be worth the friction.

02 · Research

What the evidence
actually says.

Four findings from published work. Together they argue that the missing layer isn’t another sensor — it’s interpretation against a personal baseline.

69 bpm

The spread between the lowest and highest normal resting heart rate across 92,457 adults. One person’s healthy is another person’s alarming.

Quer et al., PLOS ONE, 2020

≤ 10%

How much of that spread age, sex, BMI and sleep duration explain combined. Population norms cannot tell you what is normal for you.

Quer et al., PLOS ONE, 2020

3 bpm

Median week-to-week variation within one person. Individuals are remarkably stable — which is exactly what makes deviation meaningful.

Quer et al., PLOS ONE, 2020

29–52%

How often six consumer wearables correctly identified wake against clinical polysomnography — while detecting sleep 92–96% of the time.

Schyvens et al., SLEEP Advances, 2025

Reading 1

The population average is the wrong unit

Two healthy adults can differ by 69 bpm at rest, and demographics explain almost none of it. Yet the same adult varies by only about 3 bpm week to week. The signal is not in where you sit against everyone else — it’s in where you sit against yourself last month.

Reading 2

Confidence is not uniform, so it should be shown

Sleep-stage agreement with clinical polysomnography ranged from κ 0.21 to 0.53 — fair to moderate. These devices know reliably that you slept. They are markedly less reliable about how. A product that presents both with equal certainty is misrepresenting itself.

Where this evidence is thin

The abandonment figure most often quoted — 29% of smartwatches, 30% of fitness trackers — comes from a Gartner survey of 9,592 people conducted in 2016. It is a decade old and predates most modern health features. It is cited here as directional, not current.

No primary user research has been conducted for this concept. The user-need claims below are reasoned from the published work above, not from interviews. Validating them with real participants is the first thing this project should do next.

  2. Schyvens A-M, Peters B, Van Oost NC, et al. A performance validation of six commercial wrist-worn wearable sleep-tracking devices for sleep stage scoring compared to polysomnography. SLEEP Advances 6(2): zpaf021, 2025. doi:10.1093/sleepadvances/zpaf021
  3. Apple Inc. Apple Watch — Battery. Manufacturer specification; testing conducted July–August 2025. apple.com/watch/battery
  4. Gartner Inc. 2016 Personal Technologies Study, 9,592 respondents across Australia, the US and the UK. Reported abandonment: smartwatches 29%, fitness trackers 30%.

03 · Vision

Four questions.
Nothing else.

Most health apps answer a fifth question nobody asked: what are all your numbers? This one is built to answer only these, in this order.

  1. 01

    How am I today?

    One state, stated plainly, before any metric is shown.

  2. 02

    What changed?

    Only deviations from your own baseline. If nothing moved, say nothing.

  3. 03

    Why?

    The contributing signals, named, with how confident the system is in each.

  4. 04

    What should I do?

    One recommendation. Never a list. Never a diagnosis.

04 · Ecosystem

The advantage isn’t a device.
It’s having several.

No single product needs to cover the whole day. Coverage is a property of the ecosystem — if the handoffs between devices are treated as a designed behaviour rather than an accident.

Apple Watch

The primary instrument. Highest fidelity, broadest sensor set, and the anchor every other signal is calibrated against.

Anchor
iPhone

Always present even when nothing is worn. Movement, routine, screen patterns, location context — low resolution, near-total coverage.

Continuity
AirPods

In-ear during workouts, commutes and calls. Already positioned at one of the better sites on the body for heart-rate and temperature sensing.

Opportunistic
Passive companion

An unresolved concept — see below. Intended to cover the window when nothing else is worn, without becoming another thing to charge.

05 · Continuous Health

One record,
assembled from many.

Continuous Health is the layer that stitches partial signals into a single continuous account of a person — and is honest about which parts are inferred.

Principle 01

Continuity over isolated tracking

A gap is a first-class fact. The system knows what it doesn’t know, marks it, and never quietly interpolates across it as though it were measured.

Principle 02

Insights before dashboards

The default view is a sentence, not a grid. Numbers exist, one level down, for the moments you want them.

Principle 03

Personal baseline before averages

Everything is expressed relative to your own history. Population norms appear only where they are genuinely informative, and are labelled as such.

Principle 04

Calm technology

Silence is the default state. The system earns each interruption, and a day with nothing to report produces nothing to read.

Principle 05

Privacy by design

Baseline modelling and inference run on-device. What leaves the device is stated in plain language, per signal, and is off unless turned on.

Principle 06

Confidence is part of the answer

Every inference carries how sure the system is. Where the evidence is fair rather than strong, the interface says so instead of rounding up.

06 · Intelligence

It explains.
It never diagnoses.

The hard constraint is regulatory as much as ethical: interpretation of wellness signals is a different category from medical diagnosis, and the line has to be visible in the interface, not just the terms of service.

Learns your baseline

Four to six weeks of your own data before any deviation is called meaningful. Until then the system says it is still learning.

Explains its reasoning

Every recommendation names the signals behind it. No unexplained score movements.

Shows its confidence

Derived from measurement reliability. Sleep duration is reported with more certainty than sleep stage, because the evidence supports exactly that difference.

Defers to clinicians

Sustained anomalies produce a prompt to seek care and an exportable summary — never a conclusion.

07 · Hardware

The companion,
and its paradox.

The concept proposes a lightweight passive wearable to cover the window when the Watch is off. Stated plainly, that idea has a hole in it.

Unresolved — stated rather than hidden

A companion that senses needs a power source. A power source needs charging. Charging reintroduces exactly the gap the companion exists to close — only now across two devices instead of one.

There are credible ways out: multi-week battery at drastically reduced sampling, a two-piece design where one half charges while the other is worn, or harvesting rather than storing power. Each carries a real cost in fidelity, price, or bulk. None has been chosen here.

A second constraint shapes the answer. This concept’s own competitive read gives sleep to Oura — a ring. A ring-shaped companion would meet that competitor at its strongest point, in precisely the overnight window that motivates the device.

So the form factor is deliberately undefined at this stage. Resolving the power question is what should determine the shape — not the other way round.

Answered in v1.1. The power question was taken first, as this section argued it should be: no display, coarse adaptive sampling, and a charge window scheduled to sit inside the hours the watch is reliably worn. The shape followed from that budget. → See the Band

09 · What comes next

The honest
order of work.

This is a concept, not a plan of record. If it were taken further, this is the sequence that would make it credible.

  1. 01

    Talk to people

    Primary research with real participants on removal behaviour, charging habits, and what guidance they actually want. Nothing here rests on that yet.

  2. 02

    Define the recovery model

    Name the inputs, weightings and confidence bounds. A score nobody can explain is a number, not an insight.

  3. 03

    Resolve the power question

    Settle the companion’s energy budget first. The form follows from it.

  4. 04

    Test the silence

    The hardest claim to validate is that saying less builds more trust. That needs evidence, not assertion.

10 · About

An independent
concept.

Continuous Health is a self-directed product and design exercise by Muthukumar D, exploring how a mature health ecosystem might evolve from measurement into interpretation.

It is not affiliated with Apple, was not commissioned by Apple, and describes no product Apple has announced. Apple products are named throughout because the argument is specifically about that ecosystem — the concept does not make sense stated generically.

Every quantitative claim on this page is sourced. Where evidence is missing or dated, that is marked rather than smoothed over — including the fact that no primary user research has been conducted for this concept.
